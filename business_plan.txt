THE BIG IDEA
The one number that matters is:

    verified GBP saved per site per year.

Everything else in this plan is a step toward that number:

    know the data  ->  find the waste  ->  fix it  ->  prove the savings were real.

EnerOS is not sold as "a dashboard" and not as "an AI chat". It is sold as a
machine that finds money in a business's energy bills, proves it was saved, and
keeps finding more. If a feature does not move data coverage, GBP identified,
GBP executed or GBP verified, it is not part of the core build.


WHERE ENEROS IS TODAY
Capabilities that already work:
  - Reads bills (PDF/photo upload and email-forward), extracts the figures.
  - Audits those bills for errors with a deterministic engine, evidence per
    finding, and a plain-English narration.
  - Shows live UK electricity prices (half-hourly Agile curve per region) and
    grid carbon intensity.
  - Answers questions through a grounded chatbot that narrates server-computed
    numbers, never invented ones.
  - Keeps a workspace of sites, invoices, actions and plans.

What it is still guessing at ("the gaps"):
  1. No real half-hourly meter readings. Consumption is estimated from spend,
     so the product cannot say "you used X kWh at 17:30".
  2. No bill detail. Invoices are summaries: no billing period, no MPAN, no
     line items (unit rate, standing charge, network charges, capacity charge,
     levies, VAT).
  3. No baseline. Without baseline -> intervention -> measured outcome, a saving
     can only be estimated, never verified.
  4. Savings ideas are a fixed seed list, not opportunities worked out per site.
  5. Nothing actually acts. Actions move Recommend -> Approve -> Automate, but
     nothing plans, simulates, executes or measures.
  6. Onboarding captures almost no data (business name, site count, postcode).


STEP 1 - GET THE CUSTOMER'S DATA
Without data there are no savings. Data capture is the highest-leverage build and
the hardest part of the product, so it gets the most design attention.

1.1 The onboarding wizard
A guided, resumable, step-by-step flow. Every step is skippable; drafts persist so
a customer can leave and come back.

  Step 1  Business details and site info
          name, sector, sites, postcode(s), floor area, opening hours, occupancy,
          trading days. Feeds capacity region, benchmarks and the load shape.

  Step 2  Equipment / assets
          heating and cooling, refrigeration, ovens, hot water, lighting,
          EV chargers, solar, battery - with counts and rough sizes.
          Feeds the controllable-load model and the flexibility estimate.

  Step 3  Tariff details and past bills
          supplier, contract dates, unit rates, standing charge, network (DUoS),
          capacity charge, VAT/CCL; upload or forward 12 months of bills.
          Feeds the true cost model and the history for anomaly detection.

  Step 4  Half-hourly meter data
          CSV export from the supplier, or consent for us to pull it.
          The hardest to get and the most valuable - it unlocks the whole
          optimisation and verification layer.

  Step 5  Permission to pull data automatically
          data-sharing mandate, MPAN/MSN, billing contact. Feeds automated
          ingestion, the email-forward address and eventually direct meter links.

1.2 How the wizard behaves
  - Progressive, never gated. A customer reaches "GBP identified" after site info
    plus bills (a bill-based estimate), then improves accuracy with meter data.
  - Every field explains its purpose in one line, e.g. opening hours are used to
    find low-occupancy periods where heating can safely drop 1C.
  - A value meter runs throughout and shows what each piece unlocks, e.g.
    "data confidence 62% -> add meter data for 95%; estimated saving so far
    GBP 12,400/yr".
  - Data confidence is shown per site ("bill-only" vs "half-hourly").

1.3 Data channels, in priority order
  - PDF bill upload (already built): text layer or vision OCR -> typed invoice.
  - Bills emailed to a dedicated forwarding address.
  - CSV exports from the supplier (long timestamp,kwh or wide date,p1..p48).
  - Meter data via consent, and eventually direct links to meters/AMR.


STEP 2 - WORK OUT WHERE THE MONEY GOES
Turn a bill from a single number into its parts, then judge each part.

2.1 Itemise the bill
  unit rate x kWh / standing charge / network charges (DUoS) / capacity charge /
  levies (CCL, RO, FiT) / VAT, each attributed to a period and an MPAN.

2.2 Extend the deterministic checks
  New checks on top of the existing ten, for example:
  - wrong unit rate or standing charge versus the contracted tariff;
  - charged for more capacity than was agreed;
  - billed on estimated readings for too long;
  - billed twice for the same period or meter;
  - back-billing beyond the permitted window.

2.3 Make "normal" measurable
  Compare each site against similar businesses (sector, size, region) and against
  its own history, so abnormal consumption, abnormal cost and abnormal load shape
  become measurable rather than a matter of opinion.


STEP 3 - PROVE THE SAVINGS
Build a baseline: a model of how much energy the site would have used, accounting
for weather and trading days (and whatever else drives the load). After a change
is made, compare actual use against that baseline, with a confidence interval.

Only then may a figure be called verified. Until then it is labelled estimated.
This is the difference between a claim and a product, and it is the number the
business is priced on.


STEP 4 - GENERATE SAVINGS OPPORTUNITIES AUTOMATICALLY
Replace the fixed list with opportunities computed per site, from its own data,
in four groups:

  Tariff and procurement   switch to a better tariff, correct capacity charges,
                           recover billing errors.
  Load shifting            run EV charging, heating or cooling at cheaper times.
  Efficiency               fridge seals, lighting upgrades, thermostat settings,
                           schedule changes.
  Waste and faults         high overnight base load, equipment left running,
                           failing plant.

Rules of the road:
  - The rules are deterministic - plain logic, auditable, repeatable.
  - The AI only explains and drafts; it never invents an opportunity or a number.
  - Every opportunity carries its evidence, its estimated GBP, its confidence
    and the effort to realise it.


STEP 5 - LET THE SYSTEM ACT, SAFELY
A single loop runs: observe, diagnose, propose, simulate, get approval, execute,
verify, learn.

Safety rules, built in rather than bolted on:
  - Hard limits on comfort and food safety.
  - Nothing runs without permission.
  - Everything is logged: who, what, when and why, immutably.
  - Three trust levels: just recommend, one-click approve, automate within limits.
  - A kill switch is always visible, plus a per-site autonomy scope.

Assets are reached only through an abstraction layer, so no vendor logic leaks
into the decision engine.


STEP 6 - REDESIGN THE APP AROUND RESULTS
The product leads with outcomes, not with charts.

  Home       "You saved GBP X this month; GBP Y is waiting for your approval."
             Approvals queue, data-confidence chip per site, cheapest window today.
  Savings    The main screen: opportunities with GBP/yr, confidence, effort and
             status; bulk approve; a simulate drawer per action; a verified tab
             showing baseline vs actual and the measured saving.
  Energy     Live flow and prices, with measured vs modelled clearly labelled.
  Bills      Upload, email-forward address and meter-data import in one place;
             itemised cost breakdown, findings and a challenge workflow.
  Sites      Portfolio with data quality per site.
  Assistant  The Copilot, with tool access, never able to skip approval.
  Settings   Tariff, consent, autonomy, integrations.

Every GBP figure in the product is labelled estimated or verified. No exceptions.


ROADMAP
Phase 1 (2-3 weeks)  Data foundation
                     Onboarding wizard, richer bill data model (periods, line
                     items), half-hourly meter data import, data-confidence meter.

Phase 2 (2-3 weeks)  Analysis and opportunities
                     Itemised cost breakdown, new bill checks, peer and own-history
                     benchmarks, generated opportunity list per site.

Phase 3 (3-4 weeks)  The approve, execute, verify loop
                     Baselines, simulation, approvals, execution through the asset
                     abstraction layer, verified-GBP measurement, audit log.

Phase 4 (3-4 weeks)  Automation and real integrations
                     Autonomy within policy bounds, kill switch, real equipment
                     and supplier integrations.

Phase 5              Scale: other industries, cross-customer benchmarks,
                     portfolio and multi-site optimisation.

Phases 1 and 2 are the commercial unlock: they turn the current console into
something that can find and price savings per site. Phase 3 is what lets us
charge on verified savings. Phase 4 is the moat.


PRICING AND POSITIONING
Three per-site plans - Starter, Growth, Autonomous - plus a performance fee on
verified savings and a share of flexibility revenue. The performance fee is only
defensible because savings are verified rather than asserted.


CLOSING ADVICE
Build data and trust first, automation later:

    "an agent without measured data is just an opinion."

Get the meter data, itemise the bills, build the baseline, generate opportunities
from evidence, then earn the right to act.

Working name: EnerOS
Positioning:
The AI energy manager for SMEs.
Not an energy broker.

The strategic thesis
The strongest version of this company isn't:
“AI helps SMEs understand their energy.”
It's:
“EnerOS is the autonomous energy manager for Britain's small and medium
businesses.”
The customer gives it energy data, operating constraints and permission to
control assets.
EnerOS then continuously answers four questions:
What will we consume?
What will it cost?
What should we change?
Can we automatically do it?
The UK has a particularly useful infrastructure tailwind for this thesis: around 3
million smaller non-domestic sites are in the smart-meter rollout, while
government policy is moving toward smart/advanced-meter requirements for new
fixed-term contracts from September 2027. Ofgem is also building toward marketwide half-hourly settlement, which makes granular consumption data increasingly
important to the electricity market.
If I were actually launching this, the next step would be to turn this into a 12-
month execution plan with the exact MVP, £250k startup budget, first 100
customers, pricing page, sales funnel, technical architecture and month-bymonth P&L.
